# 24 — Reference Analysis and Originality Boundaries

**Purpose:** Learn from broad genre patterns while protecting original intellectual property.  
**Status:** Research snapshot; recheck public store listing before future use  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-19

## Reference examined

**Military Academy 3D** — Google Play listing: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.slg.MilitaryAcademy3D  
The page was reviewed on **2026-10-19**. The live listing describes an academy-to-battlefront progression, training challenges, mission rewards, a military storyline and choices with consequences. The public listing also shows a stylized single-player/offline casual game and indicates ads and in-app purchases. This analysis is limited to the public listing text and visible store metadata; no source code, private analytics or direct reverse engineering was used.

The public page also contains user reviews mentioning enjoyment of the story/short challenges as well as frustration with interruptions, bugs and limited replay value. Reviews are subjective reports, not proof of universal user behavior. They are treated as product-risk signals to test, not as verified defect rates.

## High-level lessons retained

| General lesson | How Emberwatch applies it | What not to copy |
|---|---|---|
| A recruit-to-specialist progression fantasy can be easy to understand | Mara begins inexperienced and earns trust and responsibility | Exact career path, ranks, awards, dialogue, scenes or character arcs |
| Training challenges create quick player wins | Academy drills teach movement, interaction and safe target practice | Exact obstacle layouts, prompt wording, art or order of challenges |
| Short activities are useful on mobile | Missions target approximately 6–14 minutes with checkpoints | Exact mission sequences or scene layouts |
| Rewards help sustain progression | XP, ranks, optional objectives, codex and cosmetics | Currency/reward UI, exact reward curves or purchase design |
| Narrative decisions create a sense of agency | Three documented decisions have local/campaign consequences | Distinctive choice scenes, dialogue or story outcomes |
| Clear touch controls matter | Large touch targets, control preferences and accessible prompts | Interface arrangement, icons, typography, menu styling or HUD composition |

## Distinct creative identity

| Dimension | Military Academy 3D public description | Operation: Emberwatch proposal |
|---|---|---|
| World | References historical World Wars and a US Army/commander fantasy per listing copy | Entirely fictional Republic of Veyra, post-storm humanitarian crisis; no real-world military affiliation. |
| Central objective | Career/commander rise and battlefield warfare per listing | Restore emergency warning relays, protect aid corridors and preserve truthful evidence. |
| Protagonist | Listing frames an aspiring soldier who rises to commander | Mara Venn, a repair apprentice with a practical, systems-focused motivation and personal blind spots. |
| Conflict | Large battlefront and war victory framing | A limited crisis caused by infrastructure failure, institutional concealment and competing local interests. |
| Factions | Listing describes US Army alliances and enemies | Fictional Meridian Defence Corps, Veyra Civil Response Service and Greywater Mutual Front. |
| Missions | Listing references training, combat across different terrains and technology such as drone strikes | Training, relay recovery, evidence investigation, civilian escort, bounded combat and a witnessed handover. No drone-strike system in MVP. |
| Mechanics | Broad appeal of training, missions and decisions | Companion Follow/Hold/Assist; rescue/escort; a lightweight suspicion state; authenticated evidence; explicit safe checkpoint model. |
| Visual design | Public screenshots/listing can only inform high-level readability lessons | Slate coastal materials, amber signal lamps, sea-glass accents, weathered civil infrastructure and original uniforms. |
| Business model | Public listing indicates ads and IAP at review time | MVP no ads, no purchases, offline-first; future monetization requires separate decision. |
| Closure/replay | Reviewers have expressed mixed opinions about replay and interruption (anecdotal) | Five-mission self-contained ending, authored replay objectives and no forced ad interruptions in MVP. |

## IP boundary checklist

Prohibited: extracting game assets/audio; tracing screenshots; duplicating names, dialogue, story beats, character designs, proprietary UI, mission geometry, store artwork or distinctive combinations of features; implying affiliation, endorsement or license; using the reference title/logo in branding.

Permitted creative process: genre-level observation, independent usability heuristics, original mood boards from licensed/owned sources, a new story bible and original asset briefs, abstract comparative notes about onboarding, short sessions or clarity.

Before release, a human reviewer should compare screenshots and gameplay side-by-side specifically to identify accidental imitation, not to iterate toward a closer clone. Keep the comparison as a written review note.

## Research limitation

The reference store page may change after this snapshot. Store metadata and reviews do not reveal actual retention, monetization performance or production methods. No unverified numeric conclusions are drawn. Re-open the page only for future high-level research and update the date if needed.
